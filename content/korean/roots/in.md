## What 인 (人) means

인 (人, *in*) means "person". The character is a simple drawing of a person walking, which makes it one of the easiest hanja to remember. In Korean, 인 is most common as a suffix that turns a place or a job into "a person of that kind": 한국 + 인 is "a Korean person".

Korean also has the native word 사람 for "person", and you will hear it all the time. The difference is useful: 사람 is the everyday word, while words ending in 인 are more like labels or categories (nationality, job, status).

**Root:** 인 (人), pronounced *in*. **Core meaning:** person.

## How the root works

Two ways to use 인. As a suffix, it attaches to a country name to give nationality (한국인, 미국인, 일본인) or to a job or status (군인, 성인). At the start of a word it keeps the idea of "people": 인구 (people count), 인기 (people's spirit, popularity), 인간 (between people), 인형 (person shape). 

One caution: 인 is also the sound of other, unrelated roots in Korean, so not every word with 인 is about people. Check the dictionary entry if the meaning doesn't fit.

## 3 example sentences

1. **저는 콜롬비아 사람이고 한국에서는 외국인이에요.**  
   I am Colombian, and in Korea I am a foreigner.
2. **이 노래는 요즘 인기가 많아요.**  
   This song is very popular these days.
3. **서울은 인구가 많은 도시예요.**  
   Seoul is a city with a large population.

## Memory tip

Draw 人 as someone leaning forward and walking, and read it as "in": a person who is *in* a category. 한국인 is a person *in* the Korea group. A short chain also helps: 외국 (foreign country) + 인 (person) = 외국인 (foreigner).

## How to study these words

Don't memorize the ten words as one block. Take two words today, say each one out loud, and write your own short sentence with it. Tomorrow, cover the English column and try to recall the meaning from the Korean alone. When you meet a new word with 인, guess its meaning from the root first, then check a dictionary. A wrong guess teaches more than a lookup, and after a week the root starts to feel familiar instead of foreign.
