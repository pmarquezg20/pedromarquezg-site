## What 생 (生) means

생 (生, *saeng*) is a root with three related meanings: "life", "to be born" and, at the end of a word, "a person in school". The character 生 originally pictured a plant sprouting from the ground, which explains why birth and life share it.

Because it has more than one job, 생 is worth learning by position. At the end of a word that names a school level (대학생, 유학생), it means "student". In other words (생일, 생활, 인생) it carries the sense of life or birth.

**Root:** 생 (生), pronounced *saeng*. **Core meaning:** life, to be born, student.

## How the root works

Two details help. First, 선생님 is made of 先生 ("born earlier") plus the respectful ending 님. A teacher is someone who was born, or started, before you. Second, 생신 is the polite form of 생일. Use 생신 for grandparents and other elders, and 생일 for friends and yourself.

## 3 example sentences

1. **저는 한국에서 사는 유학생이에요.**  
   I am an international student living in Korea.
2. **다음 주 금요일이 제 생일이에요.**  
   My birthday is next Friday.
3. **한국 생활이 조금 익숙해졌어요.**  
   I have gotten a little used to life in Korea.

## Memory tip

Picture a seedling coming out of soil: that is the shape of 生. Say "a sprout is life, and a student is a sprout in school". The same image covers 생일 (the day you sprouted), 생활 (the way you live) and 대학생 (a sprout at university).

## How to study these words

Don't memorize the ten words as one block. Take two words today, say each one out loud, and write your own short sentence with it. Tomorrow, cover the English column and try to recall the meaning from the Korean alone. When you meet a new word with 생, guess its meaning from the root first, then check a dictionary. A wrong guess teaches more than a lookup, and after a week the root starts to feel familiar instead of foreign.
