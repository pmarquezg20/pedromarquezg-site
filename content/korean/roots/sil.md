## What 실 (室) means

실 (室, *sil*) means "room". It is almost always a suffix: the first part of the word tells you what the room is for, and 실 closes the word. A 교실 is a "teaching room", a 침실 is a "sleeping room", a 화장실 is a "makeup room" (the old word for restroom).

This is one of the most practical roots for daily life in Korea, since signs on buildings, doors and subway stations use these words all the time. Learning the pattern lets you read many of them even when you have never seen the exact word.

**Root:** 실 (室), pronounced *sil*. **Core meaning:** room.

## How the root works

Note the pair 욕실 and 화장실. Both are "bathroom" in English, but 욕실 is the room with a bath or shower, while 화장실 is where the toilet is. In many Korean homes these are the same room, so the words overlap in daily speech.

A caution: 실 is also a native Korean word for "thread" and the sound of other roots, such as 實 in 현실 (reality). A word that ends in 실 and is not about a room may be one of those.

## 3 example sentences

1. **화장실이 어디에 있어요?**  
   Where is the restroom?
2. **저는 매일 연습실에서 피아노를 쳐요.**  
   I play piano in the practice room every day.
3. **회의실은 이 층 오른쪽에 있어요.**  
   The meeting room is on the second floor, on the right.

## Memory tip

Hear "sil" as "seal" and picture a seal sitting alone in a tiny room. Every word ends the same way, so ask yourself only one question: "what is this room for?" 교 is teaching, 침 is sleeping, 사무 is office work. Learn the front half and the room comes free.

## How to study these words

Don't memorize the ten words as one block. Take two words today, say each one out loud, and write your own short sentence with it. Tomorrow, cover the English column and try to recall the meaning from the Korean alone. When you meet a new word with 실, guess its meaning from the root first, then check a dictionary. A wrong guess teaches more than a lookup, and after a week the root starts to feel familiar instead of foreign.
