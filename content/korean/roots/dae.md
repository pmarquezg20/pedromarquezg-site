## What 대 (大) means

대 (大, *dae*) means "big" or "great". Its hanja, 大, looks like a person stretching their arms wide, as if to say "this big". It is a prefix in most of the words below: it attaches to the front of a noun and makes it larger or more important.

Korean has the native word 크다 for "to be big". Words with 대 are the Sino-Korean version, and they tend to be nouns and more formal terms, such as 대통령 (president) or 대사관 (embassy).

**Root:** 대 (大), pronounced *dae*. **Core meaning:** big, great.

## How the root works

A trap worth knowing: Korean has many different roots pronounced 대. For example, 대답 (answer) uses 對, and 대표 (representative) uses 代. They are not "big". When a word with 대 doesn't seem to be about size or importance, it may be one of those other roots.

Also see how 대학 connects back to the [학 page](/korean/roots/hak): 大 (big) + 學 (study).

## 3 example sentences

1. **서울은 한국에서 가장 큰 대도시예요.**  
   Seoul is the biggest big city in Korea.
2. **대부분의 가게가 열 시에 문을 닫아요.**  
   Most shops close at ten.
3. **주말에 집에서 대청소를 했어요.**  
   I did a big cleaning at home on the weekend.

## Memory tip

Say "dae" like "day" and imagine a very big day: a *big* day is a 대 day. Then use the arms-wide picture of 大 to remember that it means "this big". If the word is a formal noun that sounds large or official, 대 is a good first guess.

## How to study these words

Don't memorize the ten words as one block. Take two words today, say each one out loud, and write your own short sentence with it. Tomorrow, cover the English column and try to recall the meaning from the Korean alone. When you meet a new word with 대, guess its meaning from the root first, then check a dictionary. A wrong guess teaches more than a lookup, and after a week the root starts to feel familiar instead of foreign.
