## The method

About half of Korean vocabulary is Sino-Korean: words built from short syllable roots that come from Chinese characters (hanja). The same root shows up again and again, so learning one root unlocks a whole family of words. When you meet a new word ending in 실, 자 or 비, you already know part of it.

You don't need to study hanja. In these materials the character is only a memory hook next to the root. The real work is the same as always: read the word, cover the translation, guess, check. The roots just make each new word cost less effort.

This is **not** a complete course, not a grammar book, and not a full TOPIK word list. It doesn't promise a score. I'm a Korean learner (not a teacher); the words were checked against the Standard Korean Language Dictionary, and if you find a mistake, tell me and I'll fix it.

### Three example roots

- **학 (學, "study, learning")**: 학생 (student), 학교 (school), 대학 (university), 입학 (admission to a school), 방학 (school break).
- **자 (者, "person who…")**: 기자 (reporter), 학자 (scholar), 환자 (patient), 운전자 (driver), 부자 (rich person).
- **실 (室, "room")**: 교실 (classroom), 사무실 (office), 화장실 (restroom), 연습실 (practice room), 병실 (hospital room).
